The National Security Challenge

Cloud Misconfiguration Remains One of the Most Preventable Cyber Risks

IBM's Cost of a Data Breach Report 2024 found that the global average breach cost reached $4.88 million, up from $4.45 million the year before, while 82% of studied breaches involved data stored in cloud environments. CISA separately warns that unprotected and misconfigured cloud services are common targets for threat actors because poor configurations can enable sensitive data theft and even cryptojacking.

Security-First Engineering Practice

The DevSecOps Security Pipeline

The diagram below groups the reference workflow into eight core stages for readability. The linked GitHub Actions configuration defines 15 stages, including scanning, approval and deployment steps. Successful execution, enforced approval and production operation require separate validation.

1 Source Code Developer commits
2 Gitleaks Hardcoded secret detection
3 Checkov IaC misconfiguration review
4 Container Build Artifact creation and validation
5 Trivy CVE and config scanning
6 SonarCloud SAST and quality analysis
7 OWASP ZAP Authorized lab runtime testing
8 Secure Deploy Approval-gated EKS delivery

Prevention Goals

  • Hardcoded credentials and token leakage
  • Terraform and container misconfigurations
  • Unreviewed image promotion into deployment paths

Detection Goals

  • High and critical package vulnerabilities
  • Static code weaknesses and insecure patterns
  • Runtime exposure such as missing headers and weak defaults

Control References

  • NIST SP 800-53 control-oriented workflows
  • CIS benchmark-style hardening expectations
  • AWS Well-Architected security practices

About

About Peter

I am a cloud and cybersecurity professional with a background in AWS, cloud infrastructure, Kubernetes security, and DevSecOps. I hold the AWS Certified Security - Specialty certification and am pursuing graduate studies in Cloud Computing Systems at the University of Maryland Global Campus.

My focus extends beyond cloud security alone. I am actively developing ethical-hacking, penetration-testing, and purple-team skills to understand how attackers think, how security controls fail, and how organizations can strengthen their defenses through detection, hardening, and remediation. I believe strong security practice connects an understanding of attacks with the ability to protect systems.

Much of my public work centers on a challenge I repeatedly see in practice. Many nonprofits, healthcare organizations, schools, and small businesses depend on cloud infrastructure to run critical services, yet often lack dedicated security teams or the resources to identify vulnerabilities before they become incidents. To help close that gap, I create open-source reference architectures, implementation guides, and practical security checklists that make secure-by-design cloud practices accessible, understandable, and actionable.

My perspective is also shaped by experience outside traditional technology roles. Working in human services supporting vulnerable individuals in regulated environments strengthened my appreciation for confidentiality, operational reliability, accurate documentation, and the consequences of system failures. That experience influences how I approach technology: protecting people, safeguarding information, and helping organizations serve their communities with confidence.

I am currently studying Information Security Fundamentals through HTB Academy and have selected the Penetration Tester path, supported by ongoing study with Arewa Cyber Warrior (ACW360). This is an active learning journey practiced in authorized labs and permitted training environments. My offensive-security learning is in progress; it is not a completed professional certification. I also participate in Kaggle competitions for research and technical learning.

My long-term interests include healthcare cybersecurity, cloud security architecture, and dependable health IT systems. I am particularly interested in security assessment, threat detection, incident response, and risk management in environments that handle sensitive information and support critical services. Health Information Management remains an area of exploration and professional interest rather than an earned credential.

I am committed to continuous learning, open knowledge sharing, and helping organizations build secure, resilient cloud environments that are practical, sustainable, and centered on the people who rely on them.

Oneida, New York.
